History of Badminton

Badminton grew from a 19th‑century pastime into a fast, global sport. Here are the moments that shaped it.

1870s

Poona inspires Badminton

British army officers stationed in Pune, India, played a shuttle-and-racquet game called Poona. When they brought the pastime back to England, it was adapted at the Duke of Beaufort’s estate in Badminton, giving the sport its modern name.

1877

First Written Rules

The Bath Badminton Club published the first official rules, helping standardise court dimensions, scoring, and equipment specifications so clubs across England could play the same game.

1899

All England Championships

The inaugural All England Open cemented badminton as a competitive sport. It quickly became the world’s most prestigious tournament, drawing elite players and introducing structured competition.

1934

International Federation Founded

Nine national associations formed the International Badminton Federation (now the Badminton World Federation). This enabled unified global governance and the launch of international team events.

1977

First World Championships

Malmö, Sweden, hosted the first official World Championships. The event created a regular stage for the sport’s best athletes and accelerated badminton’s reach into new regions.

1992

Olympic Debut

Badminton joined the Barcelona Olympic Games as a medal sport. Olympic exposure dramatically grew the sport’s popularity, funding, and grassroots programmes worldwide.

2006

BWF Era Begins

The IBF rebranded as the Badminton World Federation and introduced the Super Series tour, creating a clearer professional circuit with ranking points that drive today’s competitive calendar.
